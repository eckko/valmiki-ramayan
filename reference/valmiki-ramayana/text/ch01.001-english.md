01.001.1a | The ascetic Vālmīki asked Nārada, who was devoted to austerity and Vedic study, best among the eloquent, a bull among sages.
01.001.1b | ~
01.001.2a | Who now in this world is endowed with virtues, and who is valorous? Who knows dharma, is grateful, true in speech and firm in vows?
01.001.2b | ~
01.001.3a | Who is endowed with good conduct, who is well-disposed to all beings? Who is learned, who is capable, and who alone is pleasing to behold?
01.001.3b | ~
01.001.4a | Who is self-possessed, who has conquered anger, who is intelligent, who is free from envy? Of whom are even the gods afraid when his anger is roused in battle?
01.001.4b | ~
01.001.5a | This I wish to hear, for my curiosity is great. O great sage, you are able to know such a man.
01.001.5b | ~
01.001.6a | And having heard these words of Vālmīki, Nārada, knower of the three worlds, addressed him with "Let it be heard" and, delighted, spoke these words.
01.001.6b | ~
01.001.7a | O sage, the virtues you have enumerated are many and hard to find indeed. Having considered, I shall tell you; let the man who is endowed with them be heard of.
01.001.7b | ~
01.001.8a | Born in the lineage of Ikṣvāku, known among people by the name Rāma, he is of controlled self, of great valour, radiant, steadfast and self-controlled.
01.001.8b | ~
01.001.9a | He is wise, versed in policy, eloquent, glorious, a destroyer of enemies, broad-shouldered, mighty-armed, with a neck like a conch and strong jaws.
01.001.9b | ~
01.001.10a | He is broad-chested, a great bowman, with hidden collar-bones, a tamer of foes, with arms reaching to his knees, a fine head, a fine forehead and a fine stride.
01.001.10b | ~
01.001.11a | He is well-proportioned, with evenly formed limbs, of glossy complexion, majestic, full-chested, large-eyed, endowed with splendour and bearing auspicious marks.
01.001.11b | ~
01.001.12a | He knows dharma, is true to his word, devoted to the welfare of the people, renowned, endowed with knowledge, pure, amenable and of concentrated mind.
01.001.12b | ~
01.001.13a | He is the protector of the world of living beings, the guardian of dharma, knower of the essence of the Vedas and Vedāṅgas, and accomplished in the science of archery.
01.001.13b | ~
01.001.14a | He knows the essence of the meaning of all the śāstras, has a good memory, is quick-witted, dear to all people, virtuous, of undejected spirit and discerning.
01.001.14b | ~
01.001.15a | He is always resorted to by the good, as the ocean by rivers; he is noble, equal towards all, and ever of uniformly pleasing appearance.
01.001.15b | ~
01.001.16a | And he, endowed with all virtues, the increaser of Kausalyā's joy, is like the ocean in depth and like the Himavat in steadfastness.
01.001.16b | ~
01.001.17a | In valour he is like Viṣṇu, pleasing to behold like the moon, like the fire of doom in anger, and equal to the earth in forbearance.
01.001.17b | ~
01.001.18a | In giving he is equal to Dhanada, and in truth like a second Dharma. That Rāma, endowed with such virtues, of true valour —
01.001.18b | ~
01.001.19a | Daśaratha, lord of the earth, wished out of affection to invest that eldest son, endowed with the best virtues and dear to him, with the office of heir-apparent.
01.001.19b | ~
01.001.20a | Seeing the preparations for his consecration, his wife, Queen Kaikayī, who had formerly been granted a boon, then asked for this boon: the banishment of Rāma and the consecration of Bharata.
01.001.20b | ~
01.001.20c | ~
01.001.21a | That king Daśaratha, bound by the noose of dharma because of his true word, banished his dear son Rāma.
01.001.21b | ~
01.001.22a | That hero went to the forest, honouring the promise, by the command of his father's word and for the sake of pleasing Kaikeyī.
01.001.22b | ~
01.001.23a | As he went, his dear brother Lakṣmaṇa, endowed with modesty, the increaser of Sumitrā's joy, followed him out of affection.
01.001.23b | ~
01.001.24a | Sītā too, his wife, endowed with all auspicious marks, the best of women, followed Rāma as Rohiṇī follows the moon.
01.001.24b | ~
01.001.25a | Followed far by the citizens and by his father Daśaratha, he dismissed the charioteer at Śṛṅgaverapura on the bank of the Gaṅgā.
01.001.25b | ~
01.001.26a | Going from forest to forest and crossing rivers abounding in water, they reached Citrakūṭa at the instruction of Bharadvāja.
01.001.26b | ~
01.001.27a | Having made a delightful dwelling there, the three of them, enjoying themselves in the forest, resembling gods and gandharvas, lived happily.
01.001.27b | ~
01.001.28a | When Rāma had gone to Citrakūṭa, King Daśaratha, afflicted with grief for his son, then went to heaven lamenting his son.
01.001.28b | ~
01.001.29a | And when he had died, the mighty Bharata, being urged by the brahmins headed by Vasiṣṭha to take the kingship, did not desire the kingdom; that hero went to the forest to seek the favour of Rāma's feet.
01.001.29b | ~
01.001.29c | ~
01.001.30a | And having given his two sandals as a trust for the kingdom, Bharata's elder brother then, again and again, turned Bharata back.
01.001.30b | ~
01.001.31a | Even without obtaining his wish, touching Rāma's feet, he carried on the rule from Nandigrāma, longing for Rāma's return.
01.001.31b | ~
01.001.32a | But Rāma, foreseeing that the people of the city would come there again, single-mindedly entered the Daṇḍaka forest.
01.001.32b | ~
01.001.33a | Having killed the rākṣasa Virādha, he saw Śarabhaṅga, and also Sutīkṣṇa, Agastya and likewise Agastya's brother.
01.001.33b | ~
01.001.34a | And at Agastya's word, greatly pleased, he received Indra's bow, a sword, and two quivers with inexhaustible arrows.
01.001.34b | ~
01.001.35a | While that Rāma was dwelling in the forest with the forest-dwellers, all the sages came to him for the slaying of the asuras and rākṣasas.
01.001.35b | ~
01.001.36a | By him, while dwelling right there, the rākṣasī Śūrpaṇakhā, who lived in Janasthāna and could take any form at will, was disfigured.
01.001.36b | ~
01.001.37a | Then all the rākṣasas roused by Śūrpaṇakhā's words, Khara, Triśiras and the rākṣasa Dūṣaṇa too —
01.001.37b | ~
01.001.38a | Rāma slew them in battle, and their followers too; fourteen thousand rākṣasas were slain.
01.001.38b | ~
01.001.39a | Then, hearing of the slaying of his kinsmen, Rāvaṇa, beside himself with anger, chose as his helper a rākṣasa named Mārīca.
01.001.39b | ~
01.001.40a | That Rāvaṇa was dissuaded very many times by Mārīca: "O Rāvaṇa, enmity with him, that mighty one, is not advisable for you."
01.001.40b | ~
01.001.41a | But Rāvaṇa, impelled by fate, disregarding those words, then went together with Mārīca to his hermitage.
01.001.41b | ~
01.001.42a | Having had the two princes drawn far away by that master of illusion, he carried off Rāma's wife, after killing the vulture Jaṭāyus.
01.001.42b | ~
01.001.43a | Seeing the vulture slain and hearing that the princess of Mithilā had been carried off, the Rāghava, burning with grief, his senses in turmoil, lamented.
01.001.43b | ~
01.001.44a | Then, in that same grief, having cremated the vulture Jaṭāyus, while searching the forest for Sītā he saw a rākṣasa.
01.001.44b | ~
01.001.45a | The mighty-armed one, having slain him, Kabandha by name, deformed in form and dreadful to behold, cremated him; and he went to heaven.
01.001.45b | ~
01.001.46a | And he told him: "O Rāghava, go to Śabarī, the woman ascetic who practises dharma and is skilled in dharma." He, the destroyer of enemies, of great splendour, went to Śabarī.
01.001.46b | ~
01.001.46c | ~
01.001.47a | Rāma, son of Daśaratha, duly honoured by Śabarī, met the monkey Hanumat on the bank of the Pampā.
01.001.47b | ~
01.001.48a | And at Hanumat's word indeed he met with Sugrīva; and Rāma of great strength told Sugrīva all that.
01.001.48b | ~
01.001.49a | Then the king of the monkeys, grieved, out of affection made known to Rāma everything concerning the tale of the enmity; and there the monkey told of Vālin's strength.
01.001.49b | ~
01.001.49c | ~
01.001.50a | And Rāma then made a promise concerning the slaying of Vālin; and Sugrīva was constantly doubtful about the prowess in the Rāghava.
01.001.50b | ~
01.001.51a | But the Rāghava, to inspire confidence, flung the huge body of Dundubhi a full ten yojanas with the big toe of his foot.
01.001.51b | ~
01.001.52a | And moreover, producing confidence, he then pierced seven sāla trees, a mountain and even Rasātala with a single great arrow.
01.001.52b | ~
01.001.53a | Then that great monkey, glad at heart and made confident by this, went together with Rāma to the cave Kiṣkindhā.
01.001.53b | ~
01.001.54a | Then Sugrīva, best of monkeys, tawny as gold, roared; at that great roar the lord of monkeys came out.
01.001.54b | ~
01.001.55a | Then, at Sugrīva's word, having slain Vālin in battle, the Rāghava established Sugrīva himself in that kingdom.
01.001.55b | ~
01.001.56a | And he, the bull among monkeys, having gathered all the monkeys, sent them out to the directions, wishing to see the daughter of Janaka.
01.001.56b | ~
01.001.57a | Then, at the word of the vulture Sampāti, mighty Hanumat leapt across the salt ocean, a hundred yojanas wide.
01.001.57b | ~
01.001.58a | There, having reached the city of Laṅkā ruled by Rāvaṇa, he saw Sītā, brooding, who had gone to the aśoka grove.
01.001.58b | ~
01.001.59a | Having presented the token of recognition and reported the news, and having consoled the princess of Videha, he smashed the gateway arch.
01.001.59b | ~
01.001.60a | Having killed five army leaders and seven sons of ministers too, and having crushed the hero Akṣa, he underwent capture.
01.001.60b | ~
01.001.61a | Knowing himself released from the missile by reason of the boon of the Grandsire, the hero, of his own accord, tolerated those rākṣasas who were binding him.
01.001.61b | ~
01.001.62a | Then, having burnt the city of Laṅkā, except for Sītā the princess of Mithilā, the great monkey came back to tell Rāma the good news.
01.001.62b | ~
01.001.63a | He, of immeasurable spirit, having approached the great-souled Rāma and circumambulated him, reported truthfully: "Sītā has been seen."
01.001.63b | ~
01.001.64a | Then, having gone with Sugrīva to the shore of the great ocean, he agitated the sea with arrows resembling the sun.
01.001.64b | ~
01.001.65a | And Samudra, the lord of rivers, showed himself; and at the Ocean's word indeed he had Nala build a causeway.
01.001.65b | ~
01.001.66a | Having gone by it to the city of Laṅkā and slain Rāvaṇa in battle, he consecrated Vibhīṣaṇa in Laṅkā as lord of the rākṣasas.
01.001.66b | ~
01.001.67a | By that great deed of the great-souled Rāghava, the three worlds with their moving and unmoving beings, with their hosts of gods and sages, were pleased.
01.001.67b | ~
01.001.68a | Thus, honoured by all the deities, who were supremely pleased, Rāma, his task accomplished and free from anguish, then rejoiced.
01.001.68b | ~
01.001.69a | Having obtained boons from the deities and raised up the monkeys, he then mounted that Puṣpaka and went to Nandigrāma.
01.001.69b | ~
01.001.70a | In Nandigrāma, having given up his matted hair, the sinless Rāma, together with his brothers, having regained Sītā, obtained the kingdom again.
01.001.70b | ~
01.001.71a | The people were delighted and joyful, content, well-nourished, very righteous, free from disease and free from sickness, free of famine and fear.
01.001.71b | ~
01.001.72a | No men anywhere will see the death of a son; and women will always be unwidowed and devoted to their husbands.
01.001.72b | ~
01.001.73a | There is no danger at all from wind, creatures do not drown in the waters, and there is no danger at all from fire; as in the Kṛta age, so it is.
01.001.73b | ~
01.001.74a | Having sacrificed with hundreds of horse sacrifices and likewise with Bahusuvarṇaka rites, having given crores and myriads of cows to the learned according to rite,
01.001.74b | ~
01.001.75a | the Rāghava will establish royal lineages a hundredfold, and in this world he will set the four classes each in its own dharma.
01.001.75b | ~
01.001.76a | Having ruled the kingdom for ten thousand years and ten hundred years, Rāma will go to the world of Brahmā.
01.001.76b | ~
01.001.77a | Whoever recites this story of Rāma's deeds, purifying, sin-destroying, meritorious and equal to the Vedas, is freed from all sins.
01.001.77b | ~
01.001.78a | A man reciting this life-giving narrative, the Rāmāyaṇa, together with sons and grandsons and his retinue, is honoured in heaven after death.
01.001.78b | ~
01.001.79a | A twice-born reciting it would attain excellence in speech; should he be a kṣatriya, he would attain lordship of the earth; a merchant would attain profit from his wares; and even a śūdra would attain greatness.
01.001.79b | ~
